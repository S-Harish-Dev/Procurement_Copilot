# Procurement Fraud Investigation Procedure

**Effective date:** 2025-08-20
**Owning department:** Company-wide
**Classification:** RESTRICTED

## 1. Purpose

This procedure sets out how Acme reports, contains, investigates and resolves suspected fraud in the procurement process. It aims to stop losses quickly, preserve evidence in a form that can support disciplinary, civil or criminal action, and protect people who raise concerns in good faith.

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to all employees, managers, Finance staff and Administrators, and to suspected fraud involving vendors, contractors or third parties acting in Acme's procurement process. Examples of procurement fraud include:

- Fictitious vendors or invoices, and duplicate billing.
- Diversion of payments through fraudulent bank detail changes.
- Kickbacks, bribes or undisclosed relationships between employees and vendors.
- Bid rigging, collusion or manipulation of quotes and tenders.
- Splitting purchases to avoid approval thresholds, or approving one's own request through another user's account.
- Misuse of P-Cards or emergency purchasing for personal benefit.

## 3. Investigation Rules

### 3.1 Reporting

- Suspected procurement fraud is reported to the Chief Compliance Officer within 24 hours.
- Reports may be made directly to the Chief Compliance Officer, through a department manager, or anonymously through the ethics hotline. Managers who receive a report must pass it on without starting their own enquiries.
- The person reporting must not confront the suspected individual or vendor, alert them, or attempt to collect evidence themselves.

### 3.2 Triage

- The Chief Compliance Officer logs every report in the restricted case register within 1 business day and assigns a case reference.
- Within 3 business days, the Chief Compliance Officer, the head of Legal and a senior Finance representative (none of whom may be connected to the matter) decide whether to open a formal investigation and agree its scope.
- Cases with an estimated exposure above USD 100,000, or involving a department manager, Finance approver or Administrator, are escalated to the Audit Committee chair.

### 3.3 Containment

On opening an investigation, the following steps are taken as needed, normally within 2 business days:

- Pending payments to the vendor concerned are placed on hold by Finance.
- The vendor is suspended in the procurement system so that it cannot receive new orders.
- The procurement system and P-Card access of any employee under investigation is restricted or removed by an Administrator not involved in the case.
- Recent bank detail changes for the vendor are re-verified through a telephone call-back to a known contact.

### 3.4 Evidence preservation

- Legal issues a legal hold covering procurement system records, approval logs, emails, chat messages and device data for the people and vendors concerned.
- Administrators export audit logs for the procurement system covering at least the previous 24 months, and store them in the restricted case folder with a record of who collected them and when.
- Devices are collected and imaged only by IT security staff or an external forensic firm instructed by Legal.

### 3.5 Investigation steps

1. Review purchase requests, approval chains, quotes, receipts and invoices for the period in question.
2. Analyse the vendor master for links to employees, such as shared addresses or bank accounts.
3. Run data analytics for split purchases, round-number invoices, approvals just below thresholds and out-of-hours activity.
4. Interview witnesses, then the subject, with a member of HR present. Interviews are documented and the subject is given the opportunity to respond.
5. Prepare a written findings report for the Chief Compliance Officer and head of Legal.

Investigations should be completed within 60 days of opening, unless the triage group approves an extension.

### 3.6 Outcomes

- Substantiated findings against employees are referred to HR for disciplinary action, up to and including dismissal.
- Legal decides whether to pursue recovery from vendors or individuals and whether to refer the matter to law enforcement.
- Finance quantifies the loss and records any recovery. Control weaknesses identified are reported to the process owner, who must provide a remediation plan within 30 days.

## 4. Responsibilities

- **All employees** report suspicions promptly and cooperate fully with investigations.
- **Department managers** escalate reports without investigating themselves.
- **Chief Compliance Officer** owns the case register and coordinates investigations.
- **Legal** directs investigations, issues legal holds and manages external advisers.
- **Finance** applies payment holds, quantifies losses and supports data analysis.
- **Administrators** restrict access and preserve system logs.

## 5. Confidentiality and Protection

All case information is restricted to the investigation team. Retaliation against anyone who reports a concern in good faith is prohibited and is itself a disciplinary offence.

## 6. Exceptions

No exception may be made to the 24-hour reporting requirement. Where the Chief Compliance Officer is implicated, reports go directly to the head of Legal and the Audit Committee chair.
