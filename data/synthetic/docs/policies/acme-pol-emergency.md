# Emergency Purchasing Procedure

**Effective date:** 2025-12-01
**Owning department:** Company-wide
**Classification:** INTERNAL

## 1. Purpose

This procedure allows Acme Corp employees to obtain urgently needed goods or services when following the normal purchase request and approval process would cause serious harm. It also sets the controls that apply afterwards, so that emergency purchasing remains transparent and is not used to bypass normal procurement rules.

## 2. Scope

The procedure applies to all employees in all departments. It covers only purchases made before the related purchase request has been approved. All other purchases follow the Acme Procurement Policy and the Purchase Approval Matrix in the normal way.

## 3. Procedure

### 3.1 What counts as an emergency

Emergency purchases may be made before approval only to prevent safety risks or major outages. Examples include:

- repairing a failed fire alarm, electrical or heating system at an Acme site;
- replacing failed equipment that has stopped a production, logistics or customer-facing service;
- urgent security work after a break-in or a cyber security incident;
- restoring a critical IT system that is down for a large number of employees or customers.

The following are not emergencies: poor planning, an expiring budget, a missed contract renewal, a vendor's limited-time discount or a deadline set internally.

### 3.2 Authorising an emergency purchase

Before committing Acme, the employee must obtain verbal or written agreement from their department manager, or, if the manager cannot be reached within 1 hour, from any other department manager or Finance. The employee records who agreed and when. Emergency purchases above USD 10,000 must also be notified to Finance on the same day, and those above USD 100,000 to an Administrator on the same day.

### 3.3 Making the purchase

Where possible, the employee uses an approved (active) vendor. If no approved vendor can help in time, Finance may activate a temporary vendor for a single order under the Vendor Onboarding Procedure. The employee should obtain a written quote or confirmation of price, even if only by email from the vendor, and keep the purchase to the minimum needed to resolve the emergency. Follow-up or permanent work is purchased through the normal process.

A purchasing card may be used for an emergency purchase within the limits of the Purchasing Card (P-Card) Policy.

### 3.4 Retrospective purchase request

A retrospective purchase request must be raised within 2 business days of an emergency purchase. The request is marked as an emergency and must include:

- a description of the emergency and the risk that was prevented;
- the name of the role that agreed the purchase, and the time of agreement;
- the vendor, the amount committed and any quote or confirmation received.

The retrospective request is approved at the levels required by the Purchase Approval Matrix for its amount. Approvers confirm that the situation met the definition of an emergency.

### 3.5 Review

Finance reviews all emergency purchases each month. Departments with repeated emergency purchases for the same type of goods or services are asked to put a planned contract or stock arrangement in place.

## 4. Responsibilities

- **Employees** use this procedure only for genuine emergencies, keep records and raise the retrospective request on time.
- **Department managers** agree emergency purchases, check that they were justified and approve the retrospective request.
- **Finance** receives same-day notifications, may activate temporary vendors, reviews emergency purchases monthly and reports misuse.
- **Administrators** receive same-day notification of the largest emergency purchases and approve them retrospectively.

## 5. Exceptions and Misuse

The quote and tender requirements of the Acme Procurement Policy are waived for the emergency purchase itself but apply to any follow-up work. Legal review of a short-term emergency agreement may be completed afterwards as described in the Contract Management Procedure. Using this procedure for a situation that is not a genuine emergency, or failing to raise the retrospective request within 2 business days, is a breach of the Acme Procurement Policy and may result in disciplinary action.
