# Vendor Dispute and Litigation Guidance

**Effective date:** 2025-09-30
**Owning department:** Legal
**Classification:** CONFIDENTIAL

## 1. Purpose

This guidance explains how Acme manages commercial disputes with vendors, from the first sign of a problem through negotiation, formal dispute resolution and litigation. It aims to resolve disputes early and at the lowest cost, protect Acme's legal position and preserve evidence, and make sure that no one commits Acme to a settlement or legal action without proper authority.

## 2. Scope

This guidance applies to all disagreements with vendors about performance, quality, delivery, pricing, invoicing, intellectual property, data protection or termination, whether or not a formal claim has been made. It applies to all departments. Disputes with customers, employees or regulators are outside its scope and must be referred directly to Legal.

## 3. Dispute Handling Rules

### 3.1 Early notification

- The contract owner must notify Legal in writing within 5 business days of becoming aware of a dispute where the amount at stake exceeds USD 25,000, where the vendor has threatened legal action, or where the dispute involves a personal data breach, safety issue or intellectual property claim.
- Smaller disputes may be managed by the contract owner and department manager, but must be logged in the contract repository against the relevant contract.
- No one outside Legal may admit liability, threaten legal action or make written statements about Acme's legal position to a vendor.

### 3.2 Preserving evidence

- Once Legal has been notified of a dispute likely to lead to litigation, Legal issues a legal hold notice to the relevant departments.
- Recipients must preserve all documents and communications relating to the vendor and the dispute, including purchase requests, approvals, receipts, invoices, emails and chat messages. Routine deletion under the Procurement Records Retention policy is suspended for the held records until Legal releases the hold.
- Recipients confirm in writing within 3 business days that they have received and understood the legal hold.

### 3.3 Escalation ladder

Disputes are escalated through the following steps, unless the contract requires a different process:

1. **Operational resolution (up to 15 business days):** the contract owner and the vendor's account manager attempt to resolve the issue directly.
2. **Management escalation (up to 20 business days):** the department manager and a vendor senior representative meet to negotiate, with Legal advising.
3. **Mediation:** where the amount at stake exceeds USD 100,000, Legal will normally propose mediation before starting proceedings.
4. **Arbitration or litigation:** started only with the approval described in section 3.5.

### 3.4 Payment holds and set-off

- Finance may hold payment of disputed invoice amounts at the request of the department manager. Undisputed amounts must continue to be paid on the normal payment run.
- Acme may set off amounts owed by the vendor only where the contract allows it and Legal has confirmed the position in writing.

### 3.5 Authority to start proceedings and settle

| Value of claim or settlement | Approval required |
|---|---|
| Up to USD 25,000 | Department manager and Legal |
| Above USD 25,000 up to USD 250,000 | Head of Legal and Finance |
| Above USD 250,000 | Head of Legal, Finance and an Administrator acting for the executive leadership team |

- All settlements must be documented in a written settlement agreement prepared or approved by Legal and must include confidentiality and full-release provisions where appropriate.
- Settlement payments are raised as purchase requests in the procurement system and paid through the normal payment process.

### 3.6 Use of outside counsel

Legal decides whether to instruct outside counsel. Engagements follow the Outside Counsel Guidelines, including the requirement for a budget estimate before work begins. Legal reports the status, estimated exposure and legal spend of all active vendor disputes above USD 100,000 to Finance each quarter so that provisions can be assessed.

### 3.7 Vendor status during a dispute

Where a dispute arises from serious non-performance, fraud concerns or a breach of the Supplier Code of Conduct, Finance may suspend the vendor on Legal's recommendation. Suspended vendors may not receive new orders. Concerns about possible fraud must be reported to the Chief Compliance Officer immediately and are handled under the Procurement Fraud Investigation Procedure.

## 4. Responsibilities

- **Contract owners (employees)** identify disputes early, notify Legal and preserve records.
- **Department managers** lead management escalation and approve small settlements with Legal.
- **Legal** assesses claims, issues legal holds, instructs outside counsel and drafts settlements.
- **Finance** manages payment holds, assesses provisions and approves larger settlements.
- **Administrators** approve the largest settlements and apply vendor status changes in the procurement system.

## 5. Exceptions

Where urgent court action is needed to protect Acme's rights, for example to obtain an injunction to recover confidential information, the head of Legal may start proceedings immediately and obtain the approvals in section 3.5 within 5 business days afterwards.

## 6. Confidentiality

Dispute strategies, exposure estimates and settlement positions are confidential and may be subject to legal privilege. They must not be shared outside the people involved without Legal's approval.
