# Software Licensing and SaaS Policy

**Effective date:** 2026-03-01
**Owning department:** Company-wide
**Classification:** INTERNAL

## 1. Purpose

This policy governs how Acme Corp evaluates, buys, uses and renews software licences and software-as-a-service (SaaS) subscriptions. It protects Acme's data, prevents unmanaged "shadow IT", avoids duplicate tools and licence compliance penalties, and keeps recurring software costs under control.

## 2. Scope

The policy applies to all software and SaaS used for Acme business, whether paid or free, including desktop software, mobile apps, browser extensions that access Acme data, cloud platforms and developer tools. It applies to all employees and contractors in every department.

## 3. Rules

### 3.1 Requesting software

Before requesting new software, employees must check the IT software catalogue for an approved tool that meets the need. If no suitable tool exists, the employee raises a purchase request in the procurement system, describing the business need, the number of users, the data the software will hold and the total cost over the full subscription term.

### 3.2 Security review

All SaaS subscriptions require an IT security review before purchase. This applies to free trials and free tiers that will hold Acme data. The review assesses data storage location, access controls, encryption, vendor security certifications and integration with Acme systems. IT completes standard reviews within 10 business days of receiving the vendor's security documentation.

### 3.3 Customer data

Software handling customer data must support SSO and provide a SOC 2 Type II report. The SOC 2 Type II report must cover a period ending no more than 12 months before the purchase date. Where the software will process personal data, the vendor must also sign the Data Processing Addendum under the Vendor Data Protection Requirements before any access is granted.

### 3.4 Purchasing and approval

Software purchase requests are approved under the Purchase Approval Matrix based on the total contract value, not the monthly price. A 3-year subscription at USD 500 per month is therefore a USD 18,000 request and needs department manager and Finance approval. Multi-year commitments above USD 25,000 follow the quote requirements of the Acme Procurement Policy. Recurring subscriptions may not be paid with a purchasing card.

### 3.5 Licence management

IT keeps a central register of all software licences and subscriptions, including owner, number of seats, cost and renewal date. Each subscription has a named business owner. Licences unused for 60 days are reclaimed by IT and reassigned or cancelled at the next renewal.

### 3.6 Renewals

Subscription renewals are reviewed by the business owner and IT before the renewal date. Auto-renewing agreements are managed under the Contract Management Procedure, which requires review at least 90 days before the renewal date. A renewal that increases cost by more than 10% must be re-approved as a new purchase request.

### 3.7 Prohibited practices

- Signing up for SaaS with an Acme account or Acme data without completing the security review.
- Accepting online click-through terms for paid services without Legal review where the contract value requires it.
- Installing unlicensed or pirated software on any Acme device.

## 4. Responsibilities

- **Employees** use only approved software and request new tools through the procurement system.
- **Department managers** approve software requests and confirm the business need and seat count.
- **IT** performs security reviews, maintains the software catalogue and licence register, and reclaims unused licences.
- **Finance** approves requests above the Finance threshold and monitors software spend.
- **Legal** reviews software contracts, data processing terms and click-through agreements.

## 5. Exceptions

A time-limited evaluation of up to 30 days without Acme or customer data may be approved by IT with a lightweight review. An exception to the SSO requirement may be approved by IT for software with fewer than 5 users if compensating controls, such as enforced multi-factor authentication, are in place. There are no exceptions to the SOC 2 Type II requirement for software handling customer data.
