# Inventory and Stock Purchasing

**Effective date:** 2025-07-15
**Owning department:** Operations
**Classification:** INTERNAL

## 1. Purpose

This procedure sets out how Acme plans, orders, receives and controls inventory and consumable stock. Its aim is to keep stock levels high enough to avoid disruption to operations while limiting cash tied up in slow-moving or obsolete items.

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to all stocked items held in Acme warehouses, stores rooms and site cages, including spare parts, packaging materials, maintenance consumables and office supplies held in bulk. It does not cover IT hardware, which is governed by the IT Hardware Standards, or one-off purchases that are consumed immediately and never enter stock.

## 3. Inventory Rules

### 3.1 Item master and stocking decisions

- Every stocked item must have an item master record in the procurement system with a unit of measure, a preferred vendor, a standard cost and a storage location.
- A new item may be added to stock only when it is expected to be used at least 4 times per year. Lower-usage items are bought on demand.
- The Operations inventory team approves all new stocked items and reviews the item list every 6 months.

### 3.2 Reorder points and min/max levels

- Each stocked item has a minimum level (reorder point) and a maximum level set by the Operations inventory team.
- The reorder point is calculated as average daily usage multiplied by the vendor lead time in days, plus safety stock.
- Safety stock is set at 10 working days of average usage for standard items and 20 working days for items whose failure would stop a production line or site operation.
- Min/max levels are recalculated every quarter using the previous 12 months of usage data.
- Reorders are raised when stock falls to or below the reorder point and should bring stock back to no more than the maximum level.

### 3.3 Raising stock orders

- All replenishment orders must be raised as a purchase request in the procurement system, in line with the Acme Procurement Policy. Verbal or email orders to vendors are not permitted.
- Stock orders follow the Purchase Approval Matrix in the same way as any other purchase. The quantity ordered must not be reduced artificially or spread across several requests to stay under an approval threshold.
- Orders must be placed with an approved, active vendor. Where a stocked item's preferred vendor is suspended, the inventory team must select an alternative active vendor before reordering.

### 3.4 Blanket purchase orders

- For high-volume items, the inventory team may set up a blanket purchase order with a vendor covering up to 12 months of expected demand.
- A blanket purchase order is approved once, based on its total committed value, under the Purchase Approval Matrix. Individual call-off releases against an approved blanket order do not need separate approval, provided the cumulative released value stays within the approved total.
- When cumulative releases reach 90% of the approved total, the inventory team must review demand and, if needed, raise a new or amended request before further releases.

### 3.5 Receiving and storage

- Goods must be receipted in the procurement system on the day they arrive, so that the three-way match can proceed as described in Goods Receipt and Three-Way Match.
- Damaged or incorrect deliveries must be recorded on the receipt and reported to the vendor within 3 business days.
- Stock is issued on a first-in, first-out basis. Items with an expiry date must be issued in expiry order.

### 3.6 Cycle counts and adjustments

- High-value items (unit cost above USD 500) are cycle counted monthly; all other stocked items are counted at least quarterly.
- A full physical count of each site is carried out once a year at fiscal year end.
- Count variances above USD 1,000 per item must be investigated and explained before the adjustment is posted. Adjustments are approved by the Operations department manager and reported to Finance.

### 3.7 Slow-moving and obsolete stock

- Items with no issues in 12 months are flagged as slow-moving and reviewed for return to vendor, transfer to another site, or disposal.
- Items with no issues in 24 months are treated as obsolete. Write-offs are approved by the Operations department manager and, where the total write-off value exceeds USD 10,000, also by Finance.

## 4. Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Employee (requester or storekeeper) | Raises replenishment requests, receipts deliveries, issues stock accurately. |
| Operations inventory team | Maintains item master data, sets min/max levels, runs cycle counts and manages blanket orders. |
| Department manager | Approves requests per the Purchase Approval Matrix and approves count adjustments and write-offs. |
| Finance | Approves larger write-offs, values inventory at month end and reviews count results. |

## 5. Exceptions

Where a stock-out would create a safety risk or a major outage, the Emergency Purchasing Procedure applies. Any other deviation from this procedure requires written approval from the Operations department manager and must be recorded on the purchase request.
